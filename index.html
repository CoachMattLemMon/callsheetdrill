    How grading works

    Before showing the result, the game plays out the rest of the drive hundreds of times for every option you had and compares your choice with the best one. Green is the best call or within half a point of win chance, yellow is close (within 3), orange is costly (within 8) and red is a big miss. End-of-half spots are graded in expected points. A good call that didn't work is still a good call.

    Player mode puts you at the line as the QB: the coach's call comes in, you see the shell, and you either run it, check to one of two answers, or spike it. On a pass you then choose where the ball goes. Coach mode gives you the whole call sheet plus every clock and kicking decision.

    Your team setup drives the numbers: run game, protection, QB, kicker range, the opponent's defense and how often they bring pressure. Formations nudge the odds too: Pro I runs a little better, Empty throws a little better but gets the QB hit more.

    High school rules built in:

    • Three timeouts per half and no two-minute warning.
    • The clock stops on a first down for the chains and restarts on the ready-for-play signal.
    • Incompletions and out-of-bounds plays stop the clock until the snap.
    • A missed field goal that reaches the end zone is a touchback to the 20.
    • Play clock of 40 or 25 seconds, set in Team setup to match your state.

    The diagrams show a generic version of each concept, and every number is a model estimate built from typical high school results, not your film. Use it to argue about decisions, not to settle them.

    Import a call sheet

    Each line can be just the play name. The concept and formation are guessed from the name, and you can fix any guess before adding. Spreadsheet columns (name, concept, formation) paste in as-is.
