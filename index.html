    How grading works

    Before showing the result, the game plays your call 1,500 times against the coverage the defense showed, rolling the same dice for every option you had. Each result is valued by how likely the drive is to get the points you need from there, given yards to go, down and distance, the clock, timeouts and your team ratings. Green is the best call or close enough that the difference is luck. Yellow is close, orange is costly and red is a big miss. When your chances were small to begin with, a loss counts as a share of what you had. End-of-half spots are graded in expected points. A good call that didn't work is still a good call, and the line under each result shows what that call usually gets against that look.

    Each pass is decided by who is open. Every route is rated against Cover 0, 1, 2, 3 and 4, and combinations matter: a corner over a flat or curl, three levels to one side, two drags crossing against man, a seam clearing for a dig. Cover 2's squatting underneath defenders punish crossers and slants. Against Cover 0 and Cover 1, routes that take too long end in sacks. Runs depend on how many defenders are in the box and how the coverage fits the run.

    Player mode puts you at the line as the QB: the coach's call comes in, you see the shell, and you either run it, check to one of two answers, or spike it. On a pass you then choose where the ball goes. Coach mode gives you the whole call sheet plus every clock and kicking decision.

    Your team setup drives the numbers: run game, protection, QB, kicker range, the opponent's defense and how often they bring pressure. Formations nudge the odds too: Pro I runs a little better, Empty throws a little better but gets the QB hit more.

    High school rules built in:

    • Three timeouts per half and no two-minute warning.
    • The clock stops on a first down for the chains and restarts on the ready-for-play signal.
    • Incompletions and out-of-bounds plays stop the clock until the snap.
    • A missed field goal that reaches the end zone is a touchback to the 20.
    • Play clock of 40 or 25 seconds, set in Team setup to match your state.

    The diagrams show a generic version of each concept, and every number is a model estimate built from typical high school results, not your film. Use it to argue about decisions, not to settle them.
